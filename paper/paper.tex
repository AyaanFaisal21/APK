\begin{abstract}
Preemption on GPUs means three different operations: evicting a context
(GPreempt, Valve), manufacturing kernel boundaries so another kernel can
be scheduled (Hummingbird, LithOS), and redirecting the resident workers
of a persistent kernel to new work at safe points---the operation
ExpertPlex's serving design requires, built there on Hopper thread-block
clusters, distributed shared memory, and TMA multicast. We measure the
third operation on hardware with none of those primitives: a fully
resident persistent kernel on an NVIDIA A10 (GA102, sm\_86), with an
A100 (GA100, sm\_80) anchor, under a protocol whose claims, thresholds,
and corrective constructs were registered in public git history before
their data was collected. Findings: (1)~notification is
workload-state dependent: under an unloaded visibility floor, the
observed across-CTA maximum notification delay was one timer quantum
(1.024\us) in every one of 19{,}800 events across both parts, flat
from 16 to 432 resident blocks (elapsed ${<}2$ quanta under the
measured quantization model)---while under deliberately saturated
memory traffic a worker's set-to-observation delay reaches 97\us{}
(A10) and 3.8\,ms (A100); for the measured workload, notification is
not the
binding term (the loaded number is an observation delay, not
isolated propagation); (2)~measured handoff latency is instead
dominated by
completion of in-flight work: 54--69\us{} median across builds at tile
granularity on the saturated A10 (64.5/86.0\us{} p50/p99 in the
reference build), and its tail is predicted \emph{held-out} by an
independence model over per-block residuals---to timer resolution at
p50, p90, p95, and p99 on the A100; conservatively (measured tails
20\% lighter than predicted) on the A10; (3)~checkpointing inside a
\texttt{cp.async} pipeline lowers measured handoff to 17.4/22.5\us{}
on the A10 and 16.4/21.5\us{} on the A100---on the same silicon as the
systems literature, from user space, on a stock driver---at
whole-kernel overhead of 0--5.3\% against a registered 10\%
falsifier; (4)~across 28{,}859 forced yields with verified
buffer-conflict geometry, zero corruption was observed under both
drained and undrained shared-memory reuse (one-sided 95\% bound
$3.0\times10^{-4}$ per 10k), while a planted-fault control was caught
10{,}000/10{,}000 times; the undrained variant remains undefined under
the PTX memory model regardless---the experiment shows
non-manifestation, not permission. The one failure class that did
occur was control divergence in the check protocol, manifesting as
silent corruption rather than a hang. (5)~A tensor-core
generalization---a CUTLASS-class fp16 \texttt{wmma} pipeline at
1 block/SM---transfers the safety signature and the exactly-once
scheduler oracle whole, and falsifies the registered cost prediction
informatively: with no co-resident block to hide per-site barrier and
flag traffic, stage-granular checkpoints price at 13.7--14.7\%
(crossing the 10\% falsifier) while tile-boundary checkpoints hold
3\%, making checkpoint cost an occupancy-scoped property of site
density. Measured handoff stays residual-work dominated on both parts---its
tails were in fact smaller on the A100---while the saturated-memory
observation test degraded from 97\us{} to 3.78\,ms on $2.6\times$ the
bandwidth and $6.7\times$ the L2.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Three operations share the word ``preemption'' in the GPU literature.
\emph{Context-level eviction} interrupts arbitrary running work:
GPreempt~\cite{gpreempt} reaches ${<}40$\us{} on an A100 by repurposing
driver timeslicing through a patched kernel driver; Valve~\cite{valve}
does channel-level preemption in production. \emph{Boundary
manufacturing} splits work so a scheduler can interleave kernels:
Hummingbird~\cite{hummingbird} caps blocking at microsecond scale;
LithOS~\cite{lithos} atomizes grids for the same reason.
\emph{Cooperative in-kernel handoff} redirects the resident workers of
a persistent kernel to higher-priority work at safe points, leaving the
kernel and its state live. The three are not interchangeable: eviction
removes the serving engine itself, and Hummingbird explicitly
\emph{disables} its mechanism for persistent kernels---excluding
this paper's subject by construction.

The third operation is what ExpertPlex~\cite{expertplex} builds for MoE
serving, at tile granularity, bounded by one tile time plus one check
epoch. Its mechanism is expressed entirely in sm\_90 terms---CTA~0
polls a system-scope word and writes one device-scope word per
cluster; each cluster's leader CTA reads it at a tile boundary and
broadcasts through distributed shared memory, with an mbarrier handoff
for later warps---and its safety argument warns that ``independent
tile-boundary checks are unsafe because high-performance operations
pipeline both warps and CTAs across tiles.'' ExpertPlex is silent about
pre-Hopper hardware, and in-kernel yield itself predates
Hopper~\cite{coopkernels,mpk}. The open question is narrower: whether
fine-grained priority handoff at natural tile and pipeline-stage
checkpoints remains cheap and safe in a fully resident sm\_8x
persistent kernel with asynchronous pipeline state. We measure.

The narrowed question this paper answers: \emph{on a fully resident
Ampere persistent kernel with an asynchronous
(\texttt{cp.async}-pipelined) workload, what does cooperative priority
handoff cost, what governs its tail, and what synchronization contract
does safety actually require?} The emerging abstraction, which our
measurements support and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} states precisely,
is a \emph{condition}: cluster-level coordination ceases to be
necessary when no \emph{handoff-sensitive} dependency spans CTAs---no
shared pipeline or synchronization state that requires a
phase-coherent transition; the binding problems become CTA-local
pipeline quiescence and straggler control.

Contributions, each a measurement on registered constructs:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A baseline with the right control.} At full residency an
  urgent task waits the entire remaining drain (957\us{} median,
  $r{=}0.9934$ with remaining queue length, $n{=}2000$); CUDA stream
  priority cannot help, as NVIDIA documents---priorities do not preempt
  already-running work~\cite{cudart}. One open residency slot collapses
  the wait to 76\us{} (Section~\ref{sec:baseline}).
\item \textbf{The two numbers, cross-checked at the instruction
  level.} Independent per-block polling costs 0--3.4\% on the A10
  (worst cell across all code variants; 2.5--5.3\% on the A100) against
  a registered 10\% falsifier, and hands off in 54--69\us{} median
  across builds at tile granularity. The flag is a documented
  relaxed-atomic contract whose hot kernel is SASS-instruction-identical
  to the volatile protocol it replaced
  (Sections~\ref{sec:boundary},~\ref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{A corrected notification construct.} Per-block
  observation-delay distributions and a two-variant visibility
  microbenchmark separate notification from residual work: at the
  unloaded floor, the observed across-block maximum was one timer
  quantum in every event, flat to 432 blocks on both parts; under
  saturated memory traffic the set-to-observation delay grows with
  memory-system occupancy and was \emph{larger} on the
  higher-bandwidth part (Section~\ref{sec:visibility}).
\item \textbf{Straggler statistics, held out.} Handoff latency at
  saturation is the max of per-block residuals; an independence model
  fit on half the events predicts the other half's maximum to timer
  resolution at four quantiles on the A100 and conservatively
  (measured tails 20\% lighter) on the A10, with pairwise correlations tightly around zero
  (Section~\ref{sec:surface}).
\item \textbf{A safety result stated at its true strength.} With
  yield-site logging establishing collision geometry per event, zero
  corruption in 28{,}859 buffer-conflicting yields under two
  disciplines, against a poison control that fires 100\% of the time.
  We claim zero observed corruption with quantified statistical
  bounds, not causality: the drain could not be shown empirically
  necessary at this geometry (the paper says so), while remaining
  semantically required by the PTX completion model.
  The hazard that did fire is control divergence---the intra-CTA half
  of ExpertPlex's warning---which corrupts silently below Hopper
  (Section~\ref{sec:safety}).
\item \textbf{The adversarial alternative, measured.} A
  reserved-capacity Pareto with an urgent-size sweep: reservation is
  cheap (${\le}1.5\%$ at $R{=}32$; an apparent negative cost at small
  $R$ is confounded with per-part power sag and flagged, not claimed)
  but cliffs in
  $\lceil U/R \rceil$ waves and is denominated in resource envelopes,
  not slots---an unpinned urgent kernel at 79 registers received
  nothing from 32 reserved slots. Cooperative completion grows
  sublinearly in urgent size---$16\times$ the work costs $1.8\times$
  at p50 (22.5${\to}$41\us)---for ${\sim}3$\%
  (Section~\ref{sec:pareto}).
\item \textbf{The hostile-occupancy generalization.} A CUTLASS-class
  tensor-core pipeline (fp16 \texttt{wmma}, three \texttt{cp.async}
  stages, 56.8\,KB shared memory, 1 block/SM) transfers the safety
  signature and the exactly-once oracle whole and falsifies the
  registered stage-cost prediction: per-stage checkpoints price at
  13.7--14.7\% when no co-resident block hides the site's barrier and
  flag traffic---decomposed by measurement into an avoidable
  0.87\us{} flag round trip and an irreducible 0.24\us{} barrier
  term---while tile-boundary checkpoints hold 3\%
  (Section~\ref{sec:tc}).
\item \textbf{An sm\_80 anchor.} The decomposition
  transfers to sm\_80 (A100: stage handoff 16.4/21.5\us{} at 432-block
  saturation); two registered predictions about memory headroom were
  falsified in the informative direction
  (Section~\ref{sec:a100}).
\end{enumerate}

Constants are per-part and never blended. The study is a
characterization: two GPU models, one kernel family on the synthetic
surfaces plus one tensor-core pipeline at a single design point;
Section~\ref{sec:threats} states the limits. Code, registered claims,
summary data with the scripts that generate every table, and a run log
including every recorded fault are
public.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/AyaanFaisal21/APK}}

\section{Background}

\paragraph{Persistent kernels.} A persistent kernel sizes its grid to
the hardware; blocks never exit and pull work from an in-memory queue.
Megakernel systems compile entire model forward passes into one such
kernel~\cite{mpk}, removing every boundary a conventional scheduler
could use. In-kernel yield predates Hopper: Cooperative
Kernels~\cite{coopkernels} surrendered workgroups on OpenCL~2.0
hardware in 2017; MPK schedules tasks and events inside a single
megakernel on A100-class parts today. ExpertPlex's adaptive persistent
kernel (APK---we adopt its term) adds mid-flight preemption and
reallocation between the prefill and decode phases of one model.

\paragraph{What sm\_8x lacks, and what it does not.} Thread-block
clusters, DSMEM, and TMA multicast are sm\_90 features; sm\_8x has
none, so flag propagation crosses L2 rather than a cluster-local
network, and no \emph{hardware} couples the fates of neighboring CTAs.
Software coupling remains expressible---cooperative launch provides
grid-wide barriers, and any persistent queue couples CTAs through
global atomics---but the hardware-idiomatic cross-CTA pipelining that
ExpertPlex's safety warning describes (TMA multicast feeding cluster
peers) has no sm\_8x equivalent. Our workload shares plenty of
cross-CTA state---the task queue, the generation flag, claim
words---but all of it is handoff-\emph{insensitive}: device-scope
atomics keep it consistent under any phase interleaving, so no CTA's
transition can strand another. What it lacks is handoff-sensitive
state, the kind a multicast peer or phase-coupled pipeline partner
holds.
\texttt{cp.async}, available from sm\_80, supplies the intra-CTA half
of the hazard surface: asynchronous copies in flight into shared
memory.

\section{Registered protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The original claim file was committed on 2026-08-04; its registered
sections were never modified, and every subsequent construct was
appended as a dated amendment before its data was collected.
Thresholds, condensed (the claim file is the authority): The provenance is
the repository's public GitHub history, which predates all measurement
commits.

\textbf{N1.} Bounded tile-level preemption works on Ampere without
cluster primitives at cost comparable to Hopper, because the bound is
dominated by tile execution and the check-propagation epoch is
negligible. \textbf{A-N1 (falsifier).} The mechanism is
propagation-bound: the check epoch is comparable to or larger than
tile time, or grows more than $2\times$ from 16 to 72 resident blocks.
\textbf{Fixed thresholds.} Overhead ${\le}10\%$; epoch ${\le}25\%$ of
median tile time; every quoted p99 requires ${\ge}10^4$ events;
correctness always against a float64 reference, never another run of
the same kernel.

\paragraph{Three failure modes, three defenses.} This study
distinguishes---because it encountered all three---(a)
\emph{outcome-dependent flexibility}, controlled by registration: no
threshold moved after data; (b) \emph{construct validity}, which
registration cannot certify: two registered constructs did not measure
what their names claimed, and were corrected by dated post-registration
amendments with the registered readings co-reported
(Section~\ref{sec:method}); (c) \emph{implementation faults}, caught
by controls and instrumentation: seventeen are on the record, each
with its detection mechanism, and five of them are findings in their
own right. Registration, construct audit, and instrument controls are
different tools; conflating them is how measurement studies fail
quietly.

\paragraph{Post-registration amendments (2026-08-14, before their
data).} \textbf{A1}: notification measured as per-block
observation-delay distributions plus a two-variant visibility
microbenchmark, replacing first-observer delay, which is biased
downward in $N$. \textbf{A2}: safety requires yield-site logging and
issue-adjacent (window-forced) checkpoints; zero-failure cells use the
one-sided rule-of-three bound. \textbf{A3}: a reserved-capacity Pareto
with an urgent-size sweep. \textbf{A4}: an sm\_80 anchor with per-part
constants.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Workload.} One task is a $64{\times}64$ SGEMM tile computed
by a 256-thread block; inner dimension $K$ sets duration ($K{=}64$:
10.3\us{} solo on the A10 at 1200\,MHz, 7.8\us{} on the A100 at
1410\,MHz). A persistent kernel pulls tiles by atomic fetch-add; tasks
cycle a fixed tile grid so writes are idempotent and always
verifiable. The pipelined variant stages through double-buffered
\texttt{cp.async} chunks, one commit group per 16-deep chunk.

\paragraph{Definitions.} \emph{Handoff latency}: flag-set instant to
the last resident block reaching a yield point. \emph{Notification
delay} (per block): set instant to that block's observation.
\emph{Checkpoint granularity} is a structural property (at poll period
$P$, no worker retires more than $P$ checkpoints of old-phase work
after observing); \emph{measured latency} is a distribution; we never
conflate the two. \emph{Overhead}: whole-kernel drain-time ratio,
polling versus poll-free build---stated as whole-kernel deliberately,
because adding the protocol changes code generation (below).

\paragraph{Instruments.} All cross-block timing uses
\texttt{\%globaltimer}, which ticks in 1.024\us{} quanta on both GA102
and GA100 (measured; epoch-scale readings are reported as bounds at
instrument resolution). The set instant is device-generated (block~0
writes the flag at pre-scheduled instants and logs the timer at the
write; no clock-domain crossing); a host-set cross-check agrees within
0.6\us. The
setter's own observation is phase-locked one checkpoint behind its
write, so all latency statistics are reported setter-excluded (at
${\ge}216$ blocks the setter is never the last observer; at 144 blocks
it is, 0.4\% of the time). Full per-event, per-block observation
matrices are dumped for distribution-level analysis.

\paragraph{The flag contract.} The generation flag is a device-scope
atomic with relaxed loads and stores \emph{by design}: it communicates
only the generation value; claims go through \texttt{atomicCAS}, and
set timestamps are host-read only after stream synchronization, so
nothing is published through the flag and no acquire/release pairing
exists to serve. CUDA C\texttt{++} \texttt{volatile} carries no
inter-thread synchronization guarantee. The hot polling kernel
compiles to the same 4400 SASS instructions under either protocol, so
the contract costs nothing.

\paragraph{Verification and controls.} Element-wise float64 reference
checks on every output; NaN pre-fill so unexecuted work fails loudly;
\texttt{compute-sanitizer} after every kernel change; a planted-fault
(poison) control in every safety configuration---a zero-corruption
reading is accepted only alongside proof the detector fires; per-event
yield-site logging so collision geometry is measured, not assumed;
anomaly counters for incomplete observation sets (zero anomalies in
every reported cell); and exact scheduler invariants---per-task
execution counts, per-event urgent-completion counts, claimant
identity---closing the blind spot idempotent tile writes leave in any
numerical check. Hard gate enforcement: a run aborts on any gate failure; completion
markers are not gates.

\paragraph{Environment and clock discipline.} Four rented instances:
three A10s (GA102, 72~SMs, locks at 1200\,MHz) and one A100-SXM4-40GB
(GA100, 108~SMs, 1410\,MHz). Locks are verified by read-back
\emph{under load, per box}: the 150\,W A10 cap throttles through
nominally accepted higher locks; one A10 part sags to 1035\,MHz at its
cap under peak load (a per-part leakage difference), bounded by a
sag-proof replay to a ${\le}10\%$ effect inside the documented
cross-build band. Occupancy is a compile-time contract
(\texttt{launch\_bounds} pins 4 blocks/SM). Warmups discarded; every summary table in this paper
is generated from committed CSVs by committed scripts.

\paragraph{Exclusions, with cause.} The 72-block overhead cell on
GA102 (bistable block placement; bimodal drains 12\% apart); the poll
period axis of the \emph{overhead} surface (template variants differ
by 8 vs.\ 16 spill bytes---codegen, larger than the effect; only
period-1 columns compare across builds). The cost verdict quotes the
worst case over all variants and valid cells.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Baseline: what no-handoff costs}
\label{sec:baseline}

With all 288 residency slots occupied on the A10, an urgent tile on a
maximum-priority stream waits 957\us{} median, 1476\us{} p99
($n{=}2000$; p50 reproducible across sessions to 0.02\%). The wait
\emph{is} the remaining drain: correlation 0.9934 with remaining queue
length, slope 88.1\,ns/task $\times$ 288 workers $=$ 25.4\us/tile,
matching the independently measured co-resident tile time. Stream
priority influences pending-work selection only~\cite{cudart}. The
operative variable is residency shape: one open slot yields 76\us;
72 blocks on 72 SMs, 26\us. A baseline measured at the wrong shape
would flatter itself by up to $37\times$.

\subsection{Handoff at tile boundaries}
\label{sec:boundary}

One relaxed atomic load per block per tile boundary; each block acts
independently.

\textbf{Cost.} 0.8\% at full occupancy in the registered build; across
all builds and valid cells, the A10 worst case is 3.4\% and most cells
at co-residency read ${\le}1\%$ (the poll's L2 read hides under
co-resident warps). The atomic-flag build reads ${\sim}3\%$ at solo
occupancy because its \emph{poll-free baseline} got 16 instructions
faster---overhead here is a whole-kernel property, which is why we
define it that way. The registered ${\le}10\%$ threshold is met; N1's registered phrase
``cost comparable to Hopper'' is not reasserted, since the metrics
differ.

\textbf{Latency.} Median 54--69\us{} across builds of the same
mechanism at 288-block saturation (reference build: 64.5\us{} p50,
86.0\us{} p99 over $10^4$ device-timestamped events; cells at
${\le}144$ blocks reproduce to the timer quantum across builds and
across physical cards). The first observer reacts in one quantum; the
remainder is waiting for the slowest in-flight tile
(Section~\ref{sec:surface}). The urgent task itself completes in
${\sim}21$\us{} median---${\sim}30\times$ over baseline with matched
endpoints---because the first observer claims work while stragglers
finish.

Table~\ref{tab:surface} and Figure~\ref{fig:main}a. First-observation
delay---the registered epoch construct---never exceeds three timer
quanta at poll period~1 in any cell of either build (1.02/2.05\us{}
p50/p99 at 288 in the reference build, computed from the released
matrices) and does not grow with block count; at higher poll periods
the statistic includes polling alignment by definition. A-N1 fails
both of its registered tests---a verdict under the registered
construct, which Section~\ref{sec:visibility}'s corrected instrument
supersedes and confirms at the floor. Latency at fixed period
scales $1.90$--$2.25\times$ per period doubling at every block count,
as a residual-wait model predicts, and is dominated by the
checkpoint-residual term, growing $7.0\times$ from 16 to 288 blocks in
the reference build: under 4-way co-residency,
individual tile walls stretch from a 25.4\us{} throughput mean to a
57.6\us{} p99 (measured directly), and even the fastest of $10^4$
events waited 50\us.

Two distribution-level facts sharpen this. Doubling the polling CTAs
at constant occupancy (36${\to}$72 blocks) moves the observation
spread by exactly $1.00\times$; growth appears only as co-residency
rises. And the saturation tail is predictable \emph{held out}: fitting the
pooled per-block residual distribution on half the events and
predicting the maximum's quantiles on the other half, the independence
model matches the A100 exactly---predicted equals measured at p50,
p90, p95, and p99, to the timer quantum---and over-predicts the A10
tail by ${\sim}20\%$ at p99 (91.1 predicted vs.\ 75.8 measured).
Per-block marginals are heterogeneous (median delays span
9.2--29.7\us{} across blocks), but a per-block product model gives
identical predictions to the pooled model, so heterogeneity does not
move the maximum; the A10 residual gap therefore indicates mild
tail-lightening dependence, making the independence model a
conservative envelope there. Sampled pairwise linear correlations over
300 block pairs lie in $[-0.11, 0.04]$ on both parts. We registered
the opposite prediction---that SM-sharing would induce correlation and
break the fit---and report its failure. Independence here is an
approximation that survives held-out testing, not an established
mechanism; within that scope, the handoff tail is predictable from per-block
marginal residual distributions.

\subsection{Notification floor and loaded observation delay}
\label{sec:visibility}

The registered ``check epoch'' construct (first-observer delay) is
biased: the minimum of $N$ delays improves mechanically with $N$. The
corrected instrument asks the right question---how long until
\emph{every} block observes---with tile work removed. Two variants at
matched residency (4 blocks/SM, occupancy pinned): a \emph{floor}
(blocks only poll) and a \emph{loaded} variant (known-length FMA
cadence plus DRAM-resident streaming). We report the loaded variant as
\emph{observation delay}: it measures a worker's time to reach and
service the poll under load, including whatever stalls the worker (registered as ``loaded
visibility'').

\textbf{Floor: below the instrument's resolution, in every event.}
The observed across-block maximum delay was one timer quantum in every
one of 9{,}900 events per part---not a percentile statement---flat
from 16 to 288 blocks on GA102 and from 108 to 432 on GA100; under the
measured floor-quantization model this implies elapsed time under two
quanta (2.05\us). What this measures is device-side dissemination
from one CTA's relaxed-atomic store to hundreds of independently
polling CTAs; it is not ExpertPlex's complete check epoch, whose
hierarchy also enforces phase coherence among dependent warp and
cluster actors. The precise statement: when no handoff-sensitive dependency spans
CTAs, the cluster-coherence stage of that protocol has no
corresponding synchronization obligation, and device-side notification
among independent CTAs is inexpensive in the unloaded microbenchmark.

\textbf{Loaded observation delay: millisecond tails under memory
saturation.} With the memory system saturated, the across-block
maximum set-to-observation delay grows with occupancy: on the
A10, 7--8\us{} through 144 blocks, 21.5\us{} at 216, 97.3\us{} at 288;
on the A100, 6.1\us{} at 108 blocks, 158.7\us{} at 324, and
3.78\,ms at 432, computed from the released observation matrices:
under full-occupancy DRAM streaming the set-to-observation delay
extends to milliseconds. We registered the prediction that the A100's greater bandwidth
($2.6\times$) and 40\,MB L2 would cap its tail at 25\us; it was
falsified by two orders of magnitude. The safe
statement is the measured one: greater raw bandwidth and L2 capacity
did not reduce the saturated tail under this experiment, and within
each part the tail grows with memory-system occupancy at fixed
hardware. Identifying the mechanism (queueing, arbitration, or
otherwise) requires a load sweep with profiler counters---achieved
throughput, L2 sector behavior, atomic latency---which we have not
done; we claim the falsified prediction, not the mechanism. The two
variants bracket the real workload (64.5\us{} at A10 saturation) from
below and far above.

\subsection{Mid-pipeline handoff and the safety contract}
\label{sec:safety}

ExpertPlex's warning, read carefully, is about \emph{phase
divergence} at two levels: warps within a CTA (a TMA warp loading tile
$k{+}1$ while math warps consume tile $k$; one warp switching phases
can strand another at an old-phase barrier) and CTAs within a cluster
(TMA-multicast peers must switch together). The cluster level has no
sm\_8x analog in a workload without handoff-sensitive cross-CTA
state. The intra-CTA
level is real, and we hit it (below). A third hazard---data races
against in-flight \texttt{cp.async} groups when shared memory is
reused at a yield---is the one our experiment forces.

\textbf{The experiment.} A double-buffered \texttt{cp.async} tile;
forced yields with the claiming block interrupting its own pipeline;
the urgent tile staged through the same shared memory an outstanding
copy group targets. Yield-site logging records, per event, which stage
checkpoint claimed---so collision geometry is measured, not assumed:
at stage checkpoints the outstanding group targets the conflicted
buffer at one site in four (2{,}630 events per $10^4$); at
issue-adjacent (window-forced) checkpoints, placed before any wait, at
three sites in four (6{,}376 per $10^4$).

\textbf{Result.} Zero corrupt urgent outputs in every configuration on
both parts---stage and issue-adjacent, drained
(\texttt{cp.async.wait\_all} before reuse) and naive (no wait)---
across 28{,}859 verified buffer-conflict events in total (both parts,
both disciplines), with the
poison control caught $10^4/10^4$ in every run and background output
always float64-clean. Per $10^4$-event cell the one-sided 95\%
rule-of-three bound is $3.0\times10^{-4}$. We state this at its true
strength and no more: zero observed corruption with quantified
statistical bounds under this geometry, not safety-by-discipline---the
naive arm never failed, so the drain cannot be shown \emph{empirically}
necessary here. It is nevertheless \emph{semantically} necessary: PTX
makes \texttt{cp.async} writes visible only through the designated
completion mechanisms, so the undrained variant is an undefined
program that happened not to manifest, and wall-clock intuition is not
a synchronization contract. The timing account---claim-path latency
(barriers plus CAS, ${\ge}1$--2\us) exceeding L2-resident copy
completion (${\ll}1$\us)---explains only why no failure manifested; it
sanctions nothing. A DRAM-pressured variant (the loaded observation-delay
result shows congestion can stretch memory operations by orders of
magnitude) is the identified lever for making the undefined variant
fail. The drain discipline costs nothing measurable here---
SASS-identical hot loop, identical latency---and is the only variant
with defined behavior. \textbf{Protocol exactness.} Because tile writes are idempotent, a
numerical check cannot see duplicate execution, stale generations, or
wrong ownership. Exact invariants can: across ${\sim}185$M bounded
task executions and 40{,}000 events spanning both instruments---
including the claimer's abandon-and-restart path and the window-forced
geometry---every popped task executed exactly once, every fired event
completed exactly its urgent-queue count, and every event has a
recorded claimant. Zero violations. Stage-granularity checkpointing is
also the latency payoff: 17.4/22.5\us{} p50/p99 at A10 saturation, 16.4/21.5\us{} on
the A100---for 5.0\% and 2.5\% overhead respectively.

\textbf{The hazard that did fire.} The only silent corruption in the
study came from the check protocol itself:
branch conditions taken on live shared values let a late-drifting warp
resolve the yield branch differently from its block, splitting the
block across two \texttt{\_\_syncthreads} sites. NVIDIA documents
divergent \texttt{\_\_syncthreads} as undefined behavior; the observed
outcome---consistent with the two divergent paths contributing
arrivals to the same hardware barrier---was both halves releasing and
the block running permanently phase-shifted: whole-tile garbage with
plausible magnitudes, in roughly half of runs, detected only because
the poison gate failed on a matrix it cannot reach. The fix is a
barrier-protected snapshot ($P{<}10^{-6}$ under the prior failure rate
after 21/21 clean runs). This \emph{is} the intra-CTA half of
ExpertPlex's warning manifested below Hopper; in every observed
incident it produced silent corruption rather than a hang. The runtime contract that emerges: a uniform, barrier-protected
handoff decision, and established completion of CTA-local asynchronous
state before reuse. The evaluation contract, separately: a positive
control proving the corruption detector is live.

\subsection{Reserved capacity versus cooperative handoff}
\label{sec:pareto}

The obvious alternative to handoff is spatial headroom. We measured
the adversarial comparison on the A10: reserve $R$ slots
($R \in \{1..32\}$) against urgent jobs of $U$ blocks
($U \in \{1,4,16\}$, distinct tiles, job-completion e2e, $10^4$ trials
per cell), versus cooperative handoff with a $U$-tile urgent queue
popped by yielding workers.

Three results (Table~\ref{tab:pareto}). \emph{Waves:} reserved latency
degrades in $\lceil U/R \rceil$ waves exactly---$R{=}1,U{=}16$ returns
to drain scale. \emph{Envelopes:} reservation is denominated in
resource envelopes, not slot counts. An urgent kernel that drifted to
79 registers could not fit the 16{,}384 registers a 3-way-occupied SM
has spare and waited full drain \emph{despite} 32 reserved slots,
indistinguishable from no reservation ($1.8\times10^5$ trials of
accidental evidence; fault~13). The constraint is symmetric rather than
absent on the other arm: reservation limits urgent work to the
\emph{residual} resources of occupied SMs, while cooperative handoff
limits it to the persistent engine's \emph{provisioned} resources and
compiled repertoire---urgent work needing a different launch geometry
or a larger footprint than the persistent worker supports gains
nothing from either mechanism without redesign. \emph{The tradeoff is real, with one
anomaly flagged:} reservation's throughput cost is at most $1.5\%$
even at $R{=}32$, so it wins throughput while cooperative handoff wins
latency by $3$--$5\times$ at p99 and grows sublinearly in urgent size
($16\times$ the tiles: $1.8\times$ at p50, $2.1\times$ at p99). The
apparently \emph{negative} cost at small $R$ on this A10 is not
claimed as a result: the part in question sags below its clock lock at
peak power (its 288-block drain is 12\% slower than a healthier
card's), the A100 shows no such effect, and cross-card drain
comparisons implicate per-part power behavior; a dense residency sweep
with clock telemetry is queued before any oversubscription claim. On the A100 the same spot-check reproduces
(cooperative $15.4/34.8$\us{} p50 for $U{=}1/16$ versus reserved
$90/175$\us) without the negative-loss anomaly---GA100's saturated
grid is not oversubscribed.

\subsection{The sm\_80 anchor}
\label{sec:a100}

One clock-locked hour on an A100-SXM4-40GB (1410\,MHz verified under
load, saturation 432 blocks, same 1.024\us{} quantum), with
predictions registered first. Confirmed: occupancy shape, the
notification floor (one quantum, flat to 432), boundary handoff
55.3/64.5\us{} and stage handoff 16.4/21.5\us{} at saturation, the
full safety signature, and the Pareto ordering. Falsified, both
informatively: the loaded observation-delay tail and the co-residency
wall stretch did not improve on $2.6\times$ bandwidth and $6.7\times$
L2
(Sections~\ref{sec:visibility},~\ref{sec:surface} report both parts).
The decomposition---floor notification below instrument resolution,
tile term dominant, hazard in control flow---reproduces on both
measured Ampere parts; the constants are per-part.

\subsection{The tensor-core pipeline: checkpoint cost follows
occupancy}
\label{sec:tc}

The generalization test the registered failure-watch called the axis
most likely to falsify: a CUTLASS-class kernel---one
$128{\times}128$ fp32-accumulated tile of fp16 inputs per task, wmma
fragments, eight warps, a three-stage \texttt{cp.async} pipeline
through 56.8\,KB of dynamic shared memory---behind the same
scheduling skeleton. (CUTLASS-class, not the CUTLASS library: the
hypothesis is about the resource regime, and instrumenting the
library's mainloop would modify it anyway.) Occupancy binds exactly
as predicted, and it is the hostile shape the SGEMM instruments never
reached: 1 block/SM, saturation 72, no co-resident block to hide
anything. Gates first, as always: float64 verification of every
output tile (max rel.\ err.\ $9.7{\times}10^{-6}$ at $K{=}256$,
tolerance $10^{-3}$), poison control live on 10{,}000 of 10{,}000
events, \texttt{memcheck} and \texttt{racecheck} clean, zero spills
on every variant. Tile time calibrates to 10.9/17.3/30.2\us{} at
$K{=}128/256/512$ (40\,TFLOP/s at $K{=}512$, 1050\,MHz part).

\textbf{The registered predictions failed informatively.}
Boundary-checkpoint overhead measured 2.8--3.4\% against a predicted
${\le}1.5$\%---same order, wrong constant. Stage-checkpoint overhead
measured 13.7--14.7\% (window-forced variant 14.0--16.5\%) against a
predicted ${\le}4$\%, crossing the pre-registered 10\% stake: at this
occupancy, stage-granular polling is \emph{not} cheap, and the
synthetic surfaces understated it. The mechanism is measured, not
conjectured, by decomposition across three implementations. A
synchronous flag read at the checkpoint costs 42\%: the read is one
${\sim}0.87$\us{} L2 round trip per site, the site's block-wide
barrier converts one warp's stall into a whole-SM stall, and at
1 block/SM no co-resident work absorbs it. Software-pipelining the
read (loaded one stage ahead, published at the next site) removes the
round trip and leaves 13.7--14.7\%; the residual ${\sim}0.24$\us{}
per site is the barrier and uniform-decision machinery itself, which
nothing at this occupancy can hide. The SGEMM surfaces never saw
either term because co-resident blocks filled the stalls. Overhead at
solo occupancy is therefore a property of \emph{site density}, and
the practical trade is now a measured pair: stage-granular sites buy
9.22\us{} median whole-grid detection (p99 10.24\us) at 13.7\% drain
cost; tile-boundary sites buy 35.8\us{} (tile time plus one sample of
pipelined-flag staleness) at 3.0\%. First-observer detection sits at
2.05\us{} in both---the floor result, unchanged.

\textbf{What did generalize.} The safety signature transfers whole:
quiesced drain 0 corrupt in 10{,}000, naive mid-pipeline yield---with
un-awaited copy groups targeting the reused staging buffers in flight
at the dominant claim sites---0 corrupt in 10{,}000 on this geometry,
poison 10{,}000/10{,}000. The scheduler oracle holds exactly on both
arms: 14.3M tasks executed exactly once, every urgent tile exactly
once, zero invariant violations. Urgent end-to-end lands at
44.0\us{} median (stage sites) and 62.5\us{} (boundary), against
957\us{} for no handoff at SGEMM saturation. Claim sites are heavily
phase-clustered (one site takes 79\% of stage-arm claims; blocks stay
phase-correlated across uniform tiles), so site histograms are
occupancy evidence, not uniform samples---disclosed because it bounds
what the site log can support.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The condition, stated precisely.} For persistent
workloads in which no handoff-sensitive dependency spans CTAs---no
shared pipeline or synchronization state requiring a phase-coherent
transition---cluster-level coordination is not required for
fine-grained cooperative handoff: a device-scope relaxed atomic plus CTA-local quiescence
suffices, and the dominant tail term is the maximum residual
checkpoint interval across resident workers---not control-signal
propagation, which in the unloaded floor sits below the experiment's
timing resolution on both measured parts (and which saturated memory
traffic can inflate; Section~\ref{sec:visibility}). The necessary
coordination scope follows the handoff-sensitive dependency scope. This does not diminish Hopper's machinery; it identifies its
scope. ExpertPlex's hierarchy exists to keep TMA-multicast cluster
peers phase-coherent; remove that topology and the hierarchy has
nothing left to coordinate, while the problems that remain---straggler
tails that memory headroom does not shrink, and a check protocol that
must not diverge---are exactly the ones our faults and falsified
predictions surfaced.

\paragraph{What this buys in practice.} The measured arc on one part:
957\us{} (no handoff) ${\to}$ ${\sim}65$\us{} (tile boundaries)
${\to}$ ${\sim}17$\us{} (stage checkpoints, quiesced)---the last
inside the 2.2--25.3\us{} band of \emph{natural tile intervals}
ExpertPlex reports on 8$\times$H800, and on A100 silicon, below the
worst-case interval of that band, at ${\le}5\%$ cost, user-space, on a
stock driver. The cost term is occupancy-scoped: at 1 block/SM the
same stage-granular sites price at 13.7\% and the economical point
moves to tile boundaries (Section~\ref{sec:tc})---the trade is
between detection interval and drain cost, and both ends of it are
measured. Same-order comparisons only: ExpertPlex reports interval
bounds and aggregate scheduling overhead (${<}20$\us{} decode-side),
not a preemption-latency distribution; ours is a distribution on
different hardware and workload.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

Two GPU models, one vendor; Hopper claims are cited, not measured. One
kernel family and tile size on the surfaces, plus one tensor-core
pipeline at a single stage depth and register point
(Section~\ref{sec:tc}; a sweep of stage depth and register footprint,
and a grid-synchronized megakernel---a different safety regime
entirely---remain unexecuted). ``Saturated'' is shape-specific
(registers bind at 4 blocks/SM on the SGEMM surfaces; the 1 CTA/SM
shape is now measured directly, and what transfers is the
decomposition and the occupancy-scoping of cost, not any one
constant). The timer quantum floors every epoch statement. The
naive-discipline zero is geometry-scoped; causality is explicitly not
claimed. Overhead is whole-kernel by definition; codegen shifts of the
baseline moved solo-occupancy cells by $2$ points across builds.
Cross-build latency at saturation spans 54--69\us{} and one A10 part's
clock sag bounds a further ${\le}10\%$; margins on every verdict
exceed these bands by $3$--$30\times$. Host-side effects touch only
the reserved-arm e2e (ms scale) and the deprecated host-set
instrument. Two further gaps are open: the loaded observation-delay
mechanism is unisolated (no load sweep or profiler counters yet), and
the small-$R$ negative reservation cost is an unresolved anomaly
confounded with per-part clock sag.

\section{Related work}

\emph{In-kernel scheduling:} Cooperative Kernels~\cite{coopkernels}
(yield on 2017 hardware), MPK~\cite{mpk} (task/event scheduling inside
megakernels on A100/H100/B200), Concordia~\cite{concordia}, which
combines a persistent GPU executor with binary-inserted pause and
checkpoint hooks for fault tolerance---the nearest mechanism cousin,
studying checkpoint/recovery rather than latency-sensitive priority
handoff under full residency. \emph{Interruption mechanisms:}
EffiSha~\cite{effisha} and REEF~\cite{reef} (software preemption by
waiting or kill-and-recompute), GPreempt~\cite{gpreempt} and
Valve~\cite{valve} (driver/context level; whole-engine eviction,
patched or privileged driver), Hummingbird~\cite{hummingbird} and
LithOS~\cite{lithos} (manufactured boundaries; Hummingbird disables
its mechanism for persistent kernels). \emph{Spatial sharing:} MPS,
MIG, Green Contexts, which ExpertPlex positions against dynamic
reallocation---our Section~\ref{sec:pareto} measures that tradeoff
directly at the single-kernel scale. \emph{Sub-Hopper megakernels in
practice:} adaptive megakernels ship on Ada-class parts without
TMA~\cite{adamk}, and community megakernels run on consumer GA102
GPUs,\footnote{E.g., MegaQwen,
\url{https://github.com/Infatoshi/MegaQwen}.} which is the population
this study's condition describes.
ExpertPlex~\cite{expertplex} is throughout the object of comparison:
it bridges the megakernel and scheduling literatures on Hopper; we
measure the bridge's load-bearing assumptions on the hardware below
it.

\section{Conclusion}

On registered constructs, corrected where the registration got them
wrong and audited in public: cooperative in-kernel priority handoff
works on both measured Ampere parts without cluster hardware. Unloaded
notification sits below the experiment's timing resolution, flat in
worker count, on both architectures; measured handoff latency tracks
the distribution of residual checkpoint work, predictable held-out
from per-block marginals; the runtime safety contract is CTA-local---a uniform handoff
decision, completion established before reuse---and the coordination
obligations split cleanly: the cross-CTA
phase-coherence obligation has no counterpart when no
handoff-sensitive dependency spans CTAs, while the intra-CTA
obligation remains, and our control-divergence faults demonstrate it
directly. Straggler tails are the price,
and on these parts buying bandwidth did not pay them. For
the persistent kernels that already run below Hopper, the scheduler
inside is not waiting on hardware; it is waiting on its slowest tile.
