\begin{abstract}
GPU ``preemption'' names three different operations: evicting a
context, manufacturing kernel boundaries so another kernel can run,
and redirecting the resident workers of a persistent kernel to new
work at safe points---the operation ExpertPlex's MoE serving design
requires and builds on Hopper cluster hardware. We measure the third
operation on Ampere hardware that has none of it---a fully resident
persistent kernel on an A10 (sm\_86) with an A100 (sm\_80)
anchor---with claims, thresholds, and corrections registered in
public git history before data collection. Four findings. (1)~Notification is not the binding term:
with work removed, all 19{,}800 events saw every resident block
observe the flag within one 1.024\us{} timer quantum, flat from 16
to 432 blocks; under saturated memory traffic, observation delay
instead stretches to 97\us{} (A10) and 3.8\,ms (A100). (2)~Handoff
latency is dominated by completing in-flight work---54--69\us{}
median at tile granularity on the saturated A10---and checkpointing
inside the \texttt{cp.async} pipeline lowers it to ${\sim}17$\us{}
at ${\le}5.3\%$ overhead, from user space on a stock driver.
(3)~Across 28{,}859 forced yields with verified buffer-conflict
geometry we observed zero corruption (planted-fault control: caught
10{,}000/10{,}000); the one failure class that occurred anywhere was
control divergence in the check protocol, which corrupts silently
below Hopper. (4)~At 1~block/SM, a tensor-core pipeline falsified
the registered cost prediction: stage-granular checks cost
13.7--14.7\% while tile boundaries hold 3\%---checkpoint cost
follows occupancy. The measurements support a condition: cluster
coordination is necessary only where a handoff-sensitive dependency
spans CTAs; without one, a relaxed atomic flag and CTA-local
quiescence suffice.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Three operations share the word ``preemption'' in the GPU literature,
and they are not interchangeable. \emph{Context-level eviction}
interrupts arbitrary running work: GPreempt~\cite{gpreempt} reaches
${<}40$\us{} on an A100 by repurposing driver timeslicing through a
patched kernel driver, and Valve~\cite{valve} does channel-level
preemption in production. \emph{Boundary manufacturing} splits work
so a scheduler can interleave kernels: Hummingbird~\cite{hummingbird}
caps blocking at microsecond scale, and LithOS~\cite{lithos} atomizes
grids for the same reason. \emph{Cooperative in-kernel handoff}
redirects the resident workers of a persistent kernel to
higher-priority work at safe points, leaving the kernel and its state
live. Eviction removes the serving engine itself, and Hummingbird
explicitly \emph{disables} its mechanism for persistent
kernels---excluding this paper's subject by construction.

The third operation is what ExpertPlex~\cite{expertplex} builds for
MoE serving, at tile granularity, bounded by one tile time plus one
check epoch. Its mechanism is expressed entirely in sm\_90 terms:
CTA~0 polls a system-scope word and writes one device-scope word per
cluster; each cluster's leader CTA reads it at a tile boundary and
broadcasts through distributed shared memory, with an mbarrier
handoff for later warps. Its safety argument warns that ``independent
tile-boundary checks are unsafe because high-performance operations
pipeline both warps and CTAs across tiles.'' ExpertPlex is silent
about pre-Hopper hardware, and in-kernel yield itself predates
Hopper~\cite{coopkernels,mpk}. The open question is narrower, and it
is the one we measure: \emph{on a fully resident Ampere persistent
kernel with an asynchronous (\texttt{cp.async}-pipelined) workload,
what does cooperative priority handoff cost, what governs its tail,
and what synchronization contract does safety actually require?}

The answer the measurements support is a \emph{condition}, stated
precisely in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}: cluster-level coordination
ceases to be necessary when no handoff-sensitive dependency spans
CTAs; what remain are CTA-local pipeline quiescence and straggler
control. Contributions, each a measurement on registered constructs:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A baseline with the right control.} At full residency
  an urgent task waits the entire remaining drain (957\us{} median;
  one open slot: 76\us); stream priority cannot help, as NVIDIA
  documents~\cite{cudart} (Section~\ref{sec:baseline}).
\item \textbf{Cost and latency at tile boundaries}: worst-case 3.4\%
  cost against a registered 10\% falsifier, 54--69\us{} median
  handoff, cross-checked at the instruction level under a documented
  relaxed-atomic flag contract (Section~\ref{sec:boundary}).
\item \textbf{What governs the tail}: a corrected notification
  construct---floor at or below one timer quantum in all 19{,}800
  events (Section~\ref{sec:visibility})---and a straggler model
  whose held-out predictions match at four quantiles
  (Section~\ref{sec:heldout}).
\item \textbf{A safety result stated at its true strength}: zero
  corruption in 28{,}859 yields with per-event collision geometry
  measured rather than assumed---and the hazard that \emph{did}
  fire, control divergence, demonstrated and fixed
  (Sections~\ref{sec:safety},~\ref{sec:hazard}).
\item \textbf{The adversarial alternative, measured}: a
  reserved-capacity Pareto; cooperative handoff wins p99 latency
  $3$--$5\times$ while reservation cliffs in $\lceil U/R \rceil$
  waves (Section~\ref{sec:pareto}).
\item \textbf{Two generalizations}: an sm\_80 anchor with per-part
  constants (stage handoff 16.4\us; Section~\ref{sec:a100}), and a
  tensor-core pipeline at hostile occupancy that falsifies the
  registered cost prediction informatively---stage checkpoints cost
  13.7--14.7\% at 1 block/SM (Section~\ref{sec:tc}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}

\paragraph{Persistent kernels.} A persistent kernel sizes its grid to
the hardware; blocks never exit and pull work from an in-memory
queue. Megakernel systems compile entire model forward passes into
one such kernel~\cite{mpk}, removing every boundary a conventional
scheduler could use. In-kernel yield predates Hopper: Cooperative
Kernels~\cite{coopkernels} surrendered workgroups on OpenCL~2.0
hardware in 2017; MPK schedules tasks and events inside a single
megakernel on A100-class parts today. ExpertPlex's adaptive
persistent kernel (APK---we adopt its term) adds mid-flight
preemption and reallocation between the prefill and decode phases of
one model.

\paragraph{What sm\_8x lacks, and what it does not.} Thread-block
clusters, DSMEM, and TMA multicast are sm\_90 features; sm\_8x has
none, so flag propagation crosses L2 rather than a cluster-local
network, and no \emph{hardware} couples the fates of neighboring
CTAs. Software coupling remains expressible: cooperative launch
provides grid-wide barriers, and any persistent queue couples CTAs
through global atomics. The distinction that matters is
\emph{handoff sensitivity}. Our workload shares plenty of cross-CTA
state---the task queue, the generation flag, claim words---but all of
it is handoff-insensitive: device-scope atomics keep it consistent
under any phase interleaving, so no CTA's transition can strand
another. What sm\_8x cannot express is the hardware-idiomatic
cross-CTA pipelining ExpertPlex's warning describes (TMA multicast
feeding cluster peers), where one CTA's phase transition \emph{can}
strand a partner. \texttt{cp.async}, available from sm\_80, supplies
the intra-CTA half of the hazard surface: asynchronous copies in
flight into shared memory.

\section{Registered protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The original claim file was committed on 2026-08-04; its registered
sections were never modified, and every subsequent construct was
appended as a dated amendment before its data was collected. The
provenance is the repository's public GitHub history, which predates
all measurement commits. Thresholds, condensed (the claim file is the
authority):

\textbf{N1.} Bounded tile-level preemption works on Ampere without
cluster primitives at cost comparable to Hopper, because the bound is
dominated by tile execution and the check-propagation epoch is
negligible. \textbf{A-N1 (falsifier).} The mechanism is
propagation-bound: the check epoch is comparable to or larger than
tile time, or grows more than $2\times$ from 16 to 72 resident
blocks. \textbf{Fixed thresholds.} Overhead ${\le}10\%$; epoch
${\le}25\%$ of median tile time; every quoted p99 requires
${\ge}10^4$ events; correctness always against a float64 reference,
never another run of the same kernel.

\paragraph{Three failure modes, three defenses.} This study
distinguishes---because it encountered all three---(a)
outcome-dependent flexibility, controlled by registration: no
threshold moved after data; (b) construct validity, which
registration cannot certify: two registered constructs did not
measure what their names claimed, and were corrected by dated
amendments with the registered readings co-reported; (c)
implementation faults, caught by controls: eighteen are on the
record, each with its detection mechanism, and five are findings in
their own right. Registration, construct audit, and instrument
controls are different tools; conflating them is how measurement
studies fail quietly.

\paragraph{Post-registration amendments (2026-08-14, before their
data).} \textbf{A1}: notification measured as per-block
observation-delay distributions plus a two-variant visibility
microbenchmark, replacing first-observer delay, which is biased
downward in $N$. \textbf{A2}: safety requires yield-site logging and
issue-adjacent (window-forced) checkpoints; zero-failure cells use
the one-sided rule-of-three bound. \textbf{A3}: a reserved-capacity
Pareto with an urgent-size sweep. \textbf{A4}: an sm\_80 anchor with
per-part constants.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Workload.} One task is a $64{\times}64$ SGEMM tile
computed by a 256-thread block; inner dimension $K$ sets duration
($K{=}64$: 10.3\us{} solo on the A10 at 1200\,MHz, 7.8\us{} on the
A100 at 1410\,MHz). A persistent kernel pulls tiles by atomic
fetch-add; tasks cycle a fixed tile grid so writes are idempotent and
always verifiable. The pipelined variant stages through
double-buffered \texttt{cp.async} chunks, one commit group per
16-deep chunk.

\paragraph{Definitions.} \emph{Handoff latency}: flag-set instant to
the last resident block reaching a yield point. \emph{Notification
delay} (per block): set instant to that block's observation.
\emph{Checkpoint granularity} is a structural property (at poll
period $P$, no worker retires more than $P$ checkpoints of old-phase
work after observing); \emph{measured latency} is a distribution; we
never conflate the two. \emph{Overhead}: whole-kernel drain-time
ratio, polling versus poll-free build. Whole-kernel is deliberate:
adding the protocol changes code generation, so narrower definitions
attribute compiler behavior to the poll
(Section~\ref{sec:boundary}).

\paragraph{Instruments.} All cross-block timing uses
\texttt{\%globaltimer}, which ticks in 1.024\us{} quanta on both
GA102 and GA100 (measured; epoch-scale readings are reported as
bounds at instrument resolution). The set instant is
device-generated: block~0 writes the flag at pre-scheduled instants
and logs the timer at the write, with no clock-domain crossing; a
host-set cross-check agrees within 0.6\us. The setter's own
observation is phase-locked one checkpoint behind its write, so all
latency statistics are reported setter-excluded (at ${\ge}216$ blocks
the setter is never the last observer; at 144 blocks it is, 0.4\% of
the time). Full per-event, per-block observation matrices are dumped
for distribution-level analysis.

\paragraph{The flag contract.} The generation flag is a device-scope
atomic with relaxed loads and stores \emph{by design}: it
communicates only the generation value; claims go through
\texttt{atomicCAS}, and set timestamps are host-read only after
stream synchronization, so nothing is published through the flag and
no acquire/release pairing exists to serve. CUDA C\texttt{++}
\texttt{volatile} carries no inter-thread synchronization guarantee.
The hot polling kernel compiles to an identical instruction count
under either protocol (4400 in the build where the comparison was
made; later reworks moved the absolute count, and the guard is
re-pinned per build), so the contract costs nothing.

\paragraph{Verification and controls.} Element-wise float64 reference
checks on every output; NaN pre-fill so unexecuted work fails loudly;
\texttt{compute-sanitizer} after every kernel change; a planted-fault
(poison) control in every safety configuration, so a zero-corruption
reading is accepted only alongside proof the detector fires;
per-event yield-site logging so collision geometry is measured, not
assumed; anomaly counters for incomplete observation sets (zero
anomalies in every reported cell); and exact scheduler
invariants---per-task execution counts, per-event urgent-completion
counts, claimant identity---closing the blind spot idempotent tile
writes leave in any numerical check (Section~\ref{sec:oracle}). A run
aborts on any gate failure; completion markers are not gates.

\paragraph{Environment and clock discipline.} Five rented instances:
four A10s (GA102, 72~SMs, locks at 1200\,MHz) and one
A100-SXM4-40GB (GA100, 108~SMs, 1410\,MHz). Locks are verified by
read-back \emph{under load, per box}: the 150\,W A10 cap throttles
through nominally accepted higher locks, and one A10 part sags to
1035\,MHz at its cap under peak load (a per-part leakage difference),
bounded by a sag-proof replay to a ${\le}10\%$ effect inside the
documented cross-build band. Occupancy is a compile-time contract
(\texttt{launch\_bounds} pins 4 blocks/SM). Warmups are discarded.
Every summary table in this paper is generated from committed CSVs by
committed scripts.

\paragraph{Exclusions, with cause.} The 72-block overhead cell on
GA102 (bistable block placement; bimodal drains 12\% apart), and the
poll-period axis of the overhead surface (template variants differ by
8 vs.\ 16 spill bytes---codegen, larger than the effect; only
period-1 columns compare across builds). The cost verdict quotes the
worst case over all variants and valid cells.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Baseline: an urgent task waits the full drain}
\label{sec:baseline}

With all 288 residency slots occupied on the A10, an urgent tile on a
maximum-priority stream waits 957\us{} median, 1476\us{} p99
($n{=}2000$; p50 reproducible across sessions to 0.02\%). The wait
\emph{is} the remaining drain: correlation 0.9934 with remaining
queue length, slope 88.1\,ns/task $\times$ 288 workers $=$
25.4\us/tile, matching the independently measured co-resident tile
time. Stream priority influences pending-work selection
only~\cite{cudart}. The operative variable is residency shape: one
open slot yields 76\us; 72 blocks on 72 SMs, 26\us. A baseline
measured at the wrong shape would flatter itself by up to
$37\times$.

\subsection{Handoff at tile boundaries}
\label{sec:boundary}

The mechanism is one relaxed atomic load per block per tile boundary;
each block acts independently.

\textbf{Cost: worst case 3.4\% against the registered 10\%.} The
registered build reads 0.8\% at full occupancy; across all builds and
valid cells the A10 worst case is 3.4\%, and most cells at
co-residency read ${\le}1\%$ because the poll's L2 read hides under
co-resident warps. One build illustrates why overhead is defined
whole-kernel: the atomic-flag build reads ${\sim}3\%$ at solo
occupancy because its \emph{poll-free baseline} got 16 instructions
faster. The registered threshold is met with margin. N1's registered
phrase ``cost comparable to Hopper'' is not reasserted: the metrics
differ (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\textbf{Latency: 54--69\us{} median across builds at saturation.}
The reference build measures 64.5\us{} p50, 86.0\us{} p99 over $10^4$
device-timestamped events at 288 blocks; cells at ${\le}144$ blocks
reproduce to the timer quantum across builds and across physical
cards. The first observer reacts in one quantum; the remainder is
waiting for the slowest in-flight tile
(Section~\ref{sec:heldout}). The urgent task itself completes in
${\sim}21$\us{} median, ${\sim}30\times$ over baseline with matched
endpoints, because the first observer claims work while stragglers
finish.

Table~\ref{tab:surface} and Figure~\ref{fig:main}a give the surface.
The registered epoch construct---first-observation delay---never
exceeds three timer quanta at poll period~1 in any cell of either
build (1.02/2.05\us{} p50/p99 at 288 blocks in the reference build,
computed from the released matrices) and does not grow with block
count; at higher poll periods the statistic includes polling
alignment by definition. \textbf{A-N1 fails both of its registered
tests.} That verdict is under the registered construct;
Section~\ref{sec:visibility}'s corrected instrument supersedes it and
confirms it at the floor.

What latency growth there is belongs to the checkpoint-residual
term. Latency at fixed block count scales $1.90$--$2.25\times$ per
poll-period doubling, as a residual-wait model predicts, and grows
$7.0\times$ from 16 to 288 blocks in the reference build. The cause
is measured directly: under 4-way co-residency, individual tile walls
stretch from a 25.4\us{} throughput mean to a 57.6\us{} p99, and even
the fastest of $10^4$ saturation events waited 50\us.

\subsection{Straggler statistics, held out}
\label{sec:heldout}

Two distribution-level facts pin the mechanism. First, doubling the
polling CTAs at constant occupancy (36${\to}$72 blocks) moves the
observation spread by exactly $1.00\times$; growth appears only as
co-residency rises. Second, the saturation tail is predictable
\emph{held out}: fitting the pooled per-block residual distribution
on half the events and predicting the maximum's quantiles on the
other half, the independence model matches the A100 exactly---
predicted equals measured at p50, p90, p95, and p99, to the timer
quantum---and over-predicts the A10 tail by ${\sim}20\%$ at p99
(91.1 predicted vs.\ 75.8 measured).

Per-block marginals are heterogeneous (median delays span
9.2--29.7\us{} across blocks), but a per-block product model gives
predictions identical to the pooled model, so heterogeneity does not
move the maximum; the A10 gap therefore indicates mild
tail-lightening dependence, making the independence model a
conservative envelope there. Sampled pairwise correlations over 300
block pairs lie in $[-0.11, 0.04]$ on both parts. We registered the
opposite prediction---that SM sharing would induce correlation and
break the fit---and report its failure. Independence here is an
approximation that survives held-out testing, not an established
mechanism; within that scope, the handoff tail is predictable from
per-block marginal residual distributions.

\subsection{Notification: free at the floor, congestion-bound under
load}
\label{sec:visibility}

The registered ``check epoch'' construct (first-observer delay) is
biased: the minimum of $N$ delays improves mechanically with $N$. The
corrected instrument asks the right question---how long until
\emph{every} block observes---with tile work removed. Two variants
run at matched residency (4 blocks/SM, occupancy pinned): a
\emph{floor} (blocks only poll) and a \emph{loaded} variant
(known-length FMA cadence plus DRAM-resident streaming). The loaded
variant is reported as \emph{observation delay}: it measures a
worker's time to reach and service the poll under load, including
whatever stalls the worker.

\textbf{Floor: below the instrument's resolution, in every event.}
The observed across-block maximum delay was one timer quantum in
every one of 9{,}900 events per part---not a percentile
statement---flat from 16 to 288 blocks on GA102 and from 108 to 432
on GA100; under the measured floor-quantization model this implies
elapsed time under two quanta (2.05\us). What this measures is
device-side dissemination from one CTA's relaxed-atomic store to
hundreds of independently polling CTAs. It is not ExpertPlex's
complete check epoch, whose hierarchy also enforces phase coherence
among dependent warp and cluster actors; when no handoff-sensitive
dependency spans CTAs, that coherence stage has no corresponding
obligation, and device-side notification among independent CTAs is
inexpensive in the unloaded microbenchmark.

\textbf{Loaded: millisecond tails under memory saturation.} With the
memory system saturated, the across-block maximum set-to-observation
delay grows with occupancy: on the A10, 7--8\us{} through 144 blocks,
21.5\us{} at 216, 97.3\us{} at 288; on the A100, 6.1\us{} at 108
blocks, 158.7\us{} at 324, and 3.78\,ms at 432. We registered the
prediction that the A100's greater bandwidth ($2.6\times$) and
40\,MB L2 would cap its tail at 25\us; it was falsified by two orders
of magnitude. The safe statement is the measured one: greater raw
bandwidth and L2 capacity did not reduce the saturated tail under
this experiment, and within each part the tail grows with
memory-system occupancy at fixed hardware. Identifying the mechanism
(queueing, arbitration, or otherwise) requires a load sweep with
profiler counters, which we have not done; we claim the falsified
prediction, not the mechanism. The two variants bracket the real
workload (64.5\us{} at A10 saturation) from below and far above.

\subsection{Mid-pipeline yields: zero corruption at measured
geometry}
\label{sec:safety}

ExpertPlex's warning, read carefully, is about \emph{phase
divergence} at two levels: warps within a CTA (a TMA warp loading
tile $k{+}1$ while math warps consume tile $k$; one warp switching
phases can strand another at an old-phase barrier) and CTAs within a
cluster (TMA-multicast peers must switch together). The cluster level
has no sm\_8x analog in a workload without handoff-sensitive
cross-CTA state. The intra-CTA level is real, and we hit it
(Section~\ref{sec:hazard}). A third hazard---data races against
in-flight \texttt{cp.async} groups when shared memory is reused at a
yield---is the one this experiment forces.

\textbf{The experiment.} A double-buffered \texttt{cp.async} tile;
forced yields with the claiming block interrupting its own pipeline;
the urgent tile staged through the same shared memory an outstanding
copy group targets. Yield-site logging records, per event, which
stage checkpoint claimed, so collision geometry is measured rather
than assumed: at stage checkpoints the outstanding group targets the
conflicted buffer at one site in four (2{,}630 events per $10^4$); at
issue-adjacent (window-forced) checkpoints, placed before any wait,
at three sites in four (6{,}376 per $10^4$).

\textbf{Result: zero corrupt outputs, everywhere.} Across 28{,}859
verified buffer-conflict events in total---both parts, stage and
issue-adjacent sites, drained (\texttt{cp.async.wait\_all} before
reuse) and naive (no wait)---no urgent output was ever corrupt, with
the poison control caught $10^4/10^4$ in every run and background
output always float64-clean. Per $10^4$-event cell the one-sided 95\%
rule-of-three bound is $3.0\times10^{-4}$.

We state this at its true strength and no more: zero observed
corruption with quantified bounds under this geometry, not
safety-by-discipline. The naive arm never failed, so the drain cannot
be shown \emph{empirically} necessary here. It is nevertheless
\emph{semantically} necessary: PTX makes \texttt{cp.async} writes
visible only through the designated completion mechanisms, so the
undrained variant is an undefined program that happened not to
manifest, and wall-clock intuition is not a synchronization contract.
The timing account---claim-path latency (barriers plus CAS,
${\ge}1$--2\us) exceeding L2-resident copy completion
(${\ll}1$\us)---explains only why no failure manifested; it sanctions
nothing. A DRAM-pressured variant is the identified lever for making
the undefined variant fail, since the loaded observation-delay result
shows congestion can stretch memory operations by orders of
magnitude. The drain discipline costs nothing measurable here
(SASS-identical hot loop, identical latency) and is the only variant
with defined behavior.

Stage-granularity checkpointing is also the latency payoff:
17.4/22.5\us{} p50/p99 at A10 saturation and 16.4/21.5\us{} on the
A100, for 5.0\% and 2.5\% overhead respectively.

\subsection{The hazard that did fire: control divergence}
\label{sec:hazard}

The only silent corruption in the study came from the check protocol
itself. Branch conditions taken on live shared values let a
late-drifting warp resolve the yield branch differently from its
block, splitting the block across two \texttt{\_\_syncthreads} sites.
NVIDIA documents divergent \texttt{\_\_syncthreads} as undefined
behavior; the observed outcome---consistent with the two divergent
paths contributing arrivals to the same hardware barrier---was both
halves releasing and the block running permanently phase-shifted:
whole-tile garbage with plausible magnitudes, in roughly half of
runs, detected only because the poison gate failed on a matrix it
cannot reach. The fix is a barrier-protected snapshot
($P{<}10^{-6}$ under the prior failure rate after 21/21 clean runs).

This \emph{is} the intra-CTA half of ExpertPlex's warning manifested
below Hopper, and in every observed incident it produced silent
corruption rather than a hang. The runtime contract that emerges: a
uniform, barrier-protected handoff decision, and established
completion of CTA-local asynchronous state before reuse. The
evaluation contract, separately: a positive control proving the
corruption detector is live.

\subsection{Protocol exactness: the scheduler oracle}
\label{sec:oracle}

Because tile writes are idempotent, a numerical check cannot see
duplicate execution, stale generations, or wrong ownership. Exact
invariants can. Across ${\sim}185$M bounded task executions and
40{,}000 events spanning both instruments---including the claimer's
abandon-and-restart path and the window-forced geometry---every
popped task executed exactly once, every fired event completed
exactly its urgent-queue count, and every event has a recorded
claimant. Zero violations.

\subsection{Reserved capacity versus cooperative handoff}
\label{sec:pareto}

The obvious alternative to handoff is spatial headroom. We measured
the adversarial comparison on the A10: reserve $R$ slots
($R \in \{1..32\}$) against urgent jobs of $U$ blocks
($U \in \{1,4,16\}$, distinct tiles, job-completion e2e, $10^4$
trials per cell), versus cooperative handoff with a $U$-tile urgent
queue popped by yielding workers.

Three results (Table~\ref{tab:pareto}, Figure~\ref{fig:main}c).
\emph{Waves:} reserved latency degrades in $\lceil U/R \rceil$ waves
exactly; $R{=}1,U{=}16$ returns to drain scale. \emph{Envelopes:}
reservation is denominated in resource envelopes, not slot counts. An
urgent kernel that drifted to 79 registers could not fit the
16{,}384 registers a 3-way-occupied SM has spare and waited full
drain \emph{despite} 32 reserved slots ($1.8\times10^5$ trials of
accidental evidence; fault~13). The constraint is symmetric rather
than absent on the other arm: reservation limits urgent work to the
\emph{residual} resources of occupied SMs, while cooperative handoff
limits it to the persistent engine's \emph{provisioned} resources and
compiled repertoire; urgent work needing a different launch geometry
gains nothing from either mechanism without redesign. \emph{The
tradeoff:} reservation's throughput cost is at most $1.5\%$ even at
$R{=}32$, so it wins throughput, while cooperative handoff wins
latency by $3$--$5\times$ at p99 and grows sublinearly in urgent size
($16\times$ the tiles: $1.8\times$ at p50, $2.1\times$ at p99).

One anomaly is flagged, not claimed: the apparently \emph{negative}
cost at small $R$ on this A10. The part in question sags below its
clock lock at peak power (its 288-block drain is 12\% slower than a
healthier card's), the A100 shows no such effect, and cross-card
drain comparisons implicate per-part power behavior; a dense
residency sweep with clock telemetry is queued before any
oversubscription claim. On the A100 the same spot-check reproduces
(cooperative $15.4/34.8$\us{} p50 for $U{=}1/16$ versus reserved
$90/175$\us) without the anomaly---GA100's saturated grid is not
oversubscribed.

\subsection{The sm\_80 anchor}
\label{sec:a100}

One clock-locked hour on an A100-SXM4-40GB (1410\,MHz verified under
load, saturation 432 blocks, same 1.024\us{} quantum), with
predictions registered first. Confirmed: occupancy shape, the
notification floor (one quantum, flat to 432), boundary handoff
55.3/64.5\us{} and stage handoff 16.4/21.5\us{} at saturation, the
full safety signature, and the Pareto ordering. Falsified, both
informatively: the loaded observation-delay tail and the
co-residency wall stretch did not improve on $2.6\times$ the
bandwidth and $6.7\times$ the L2
(Sections~\ref{sec:visibility},~\ref{sec:surface}). The
decomposition---floor notification below instrument resolution, tile
term dominant, hazard in control flow---reproduces on both measured
Ampere parts; the constants are per-part.

\subsection{The tensor-core pipeline: checkpoint cost follows
occupancy}
\label{sec:tc}

The generalization test the registered failure-watch called the axis
most likely to falsify: a CUTLASS-class kernel---one
$128{\times}128$ fp32-accumulated tile of fp16 inputs per task,
wmma fragments, eight warps, a three-stage \texttt{cp.async}
pipeline through 56.8\,KB of dynamic shared memory---behind the same
scheduling skeleton. (CUTLASS-class, not the CUTLASS library: the
hypothesis is about the resource regime, and instrumenting the
library's mainloop would modify it anyway.) Occupancy binds exactly
as predicted, and it is the hostile shape the SGEMM instruments
never reached: 1 block/SM, saturation 72, no co-resident block to
hide anything. Gates first, as always: float64 verification of every
output tile (max rel.\ err.\ $9.7{\times}10^{-6}$ at $K{=}256$,
tolerance $10^{-3}$), poison control live on 10{,}000 of 10{,}000
events, \texttt{memcheck} and \texttt{racecheck} clean, zero spills
on every variant. Tile time calibrates to 10.9/17.3/30.2\us{} at
$K{=}128/256/512$ (40\,TFLOP/s at $K{=}512$, 1050\,MHz part).

\textbf{The registered cost predictions failed informatively.}
Boundary-checkpoint overhead measured 2.8--3.4\% against a predicted
${\le}1.5$\%: same order, wrong constant. Stage-checkpoint overhead
measured 13.7--14.7\% (window-forced variant 14.0--16.5\%) against a
predicted ${\le}4$\%, crossing the pre-registered 10\% stake. At
this occupancy, stage-granular polling is \emph{not} cheap, and the
synthetic surfaces understated it.

The mechanism is measured by decomposition across three
implementations, not conjectured. A synchronous flag read at the
checkpoint costs 42\%: the read is one ${\sim}0.87$\us{} L2 round
trip per site, and the site's block-wide barrier converts one warp's
stall into a whole-SM stall that no co-resident block absorbs.
Software-pipelining the read (loaded one stage ahead, published at
the next site) removes the round trip and leaves 13.7--14.7\%; the
residual ${\sim}0.24$\us{} per site is the barrier and
uniform-decision machinery itself, which nothing at this occupancy
can hide. The SGEMM surfaces never saw either term because
co-resident blocks filled the stalls. Checkpoint overhead at solo
occupancy is therefore a property of \emph{site density}, and the
practical trade is a measured pair: stage-granular sites buy
9.22\us{} median whole-grid detection (p99 10.24\us) at 13.7\% drain
cost; tile-boundary sites buy 35.8\us{} (tile time plus one sample
of pipelined-flag staleness) at 3.0\%. First-observer detection sits
at 2.05\us{} in both---the floor result, unchanged.

\textbf{What did generalize.} The safety signature transfers whole:
quiesced drain 0 corrupt in 10{,}000; naive mid-pipeline yield, with
un-awaited copy groups targeting the reused staging buffers in
flight at the dominant claim sites, 0 corrupt in 10{,}000 on this
geometry; poison 10{,}000/10{,}000. The scheduler oracle holds
exactly on both arms: 14.3M tasks executed exactly once, every
urgent tile exactly once, zero invariant violations. Urgent
end-to-end lands at 44.0\us{} median (stage sites) and 62.5\us{}
(boundary), against 957\us{} for no handoff at SGEMM saturation.
Claim sites are heavily phase-clustered (one site takes 79\% of
stage-arm claims; blocks stay phase-correlated across uniform
tiles), so site histograms are occupancy evidence, not uniform
samples---disclosed because it bounds what the site log can support.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The condition, stated precisely.} For persistent workloads
in which no handoff-sensitive dependency spans CTAs---no shared
pipeline or synchronization state requiring a phase-coherent
transition---cluster-level coordination is not required for
fine-grained cooperative handoff. A device-scope relaxed atomic plus
CTA-local quiescence suffices, and the dominant tail term is the
maximum residual checkpoint interval across resident workers, not
control-signal propagation---which at the unloaded floor sits below
the experiment's timing resolution on both measured parts, and which
saturated memory traffic can inflate
(Section~\ref{sec:visibility}). The necessary coordination scope
follows the handoff-sensitive dependency scope. This does not
diminish Hopper's machinery; it identifies its scope. ExpertPlex's
hierarchy exists to keep TMA-multicast cluster peers phase-coherent;
remove that topology and the hierarchy has nothing left to
coordinate, while the problems that remain---straggler tails that
memory headroom does not shrink, and a check protocol that must not
diverge---are exactly the ones our faults and falsified predictions
surfaced.

\paragraph{What this buys in practice.} The measured arc on one
part: 957\us{} (no handoff) ${\to}$ ${\sim}65$\us{} (tile
boundaries) ${\to}$ ${\sim}17$\us{} (stage checkpoints,
quiesced)---the last inside the 2.2--25.3\us{} band of \emph{natural
tile intervals} ExpertPlex reports on 8$\times$H800, and on A100
silicon, below the worst-case interval of that band, at ${\le}5\%$
cost, user-space, on a stock driver. The cost term is
occupancy-scoped: at 1 block/SM the same stage-granular sites price
at 13.7\% and the economical point moves to tile boundaries
(Section~\ref{sec:tc}); the trade is between detection interval and
drain cost, and both ends of it are measured. Same-order comparisons
only: ExpertPlex reports interval bounds and aggregate scheduling
overhead (${<}20$\us{} decode-side), not a preemption-latency
distribution; ours is a distribution on different hardware and
workload.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\begin{itemize}
\item Two GPU models, one vendor; Hopper claims are cited, not
  measured.
\item One kernel family and tile size on the surfaces; one
  tensor-core pipeline at a single stage depth and register point. A
  sweep of stage depth and register footprint, and a
  grid-synchronized megakernel (a different safety regime entirely),
  remain unexecuted.
\item ``Saturated'' is shape-specific: registers bind at 4 blocks/SM
  on the SGEMM surfaces; the 1 block/SM shape is now measured
  directly. What transfers is the decomposition and the
  occupancy-scoping of cost, not any one constant.
\item The timer quantum (1.024\us) floors every epoch statement.
\item The naive-discipline zero is geometry-scoped; causality is
  explicitly not claimed.
\item Overhead is whole-kernel by definition; codegen shifts of the
  baseline moved solo-occupancy cells by 2 points across builds.
\item Cross-build latency at saturation spans 54--69\us, and one A10
  part's clock sag bounds a further ${\le}10\%$; margins on every
  verdict exceed these bands by $3$--$30\times$.
\item Host-side effects touch only the reserved-arm e2e (ms scale)
  and the deprecated host-set instrument.
\item Open: the loaded observation-delay mechanism is unisolated (no
  load sweep or profiler counters yet), and the small-$R$ negative
  reservation cost is an unresolved anomaly confounded with per-part
  clock sag.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}

\emph{In-kernel scheduling:} Cooperative Kernels~\cite{coopkernels}
(yield on 2017 hardware), MPK~\cite{mpk} (task/event scheduling
inside megakernels on A100/H100/B200), and
Concordia~\cite{concordia}, which combines a persistent GPU executor
with binary-inserted pause and checkpoint hooks for fault
tolerance---the nearest mechanism cousin, studying
checkpoint/recovery rather than latency-sensitive priority handoff
under full residency. \emph{Interruption mechanisms:}
EffiSha~\cite{effisha} and REEF~\cite{reef} (software preemption by
waiting or kill-and-recompute), GPreempt~\cite{gpreempt} and
Valve~\cite{valve} (driver/context level; whole-engine eviction,
patched or privileged driver), Hummingbird~\cite{hummingbird} and
LithOS~\cite{lithos} (manufactured boundaries; Hummingbird disables
its mechanism for persistent kernels). \emph{Spatial sharing:} MPS,
MIG, and Green Contexts, which ExpertPlex positions against dynamic
reallocation; Section~\ref{sec:pareto} measures that tradeoff
directly at the single-kernel scale. \emph{Sub-Hopper megakernels in
practice:} adaptive megakernels ship on Ada-class parts without
TMA~\cite{adamk} (Ada-MK is evaluated on the L20), and community
megakernels run on consumer GA102
GPUs,\footnote{E.g., MegaQwen,
\url{https://github.com/Infatoshi/MegaQwen}.} which is the
population this study's condition describes.
ExpertPlex~\cite{expertplex} is throughout the object of comparison:
it bridges the megakernel and scheduling literatures on Hopper; we
measure the bridge's load-bearing assumptions on the hardware below
it.

\section{Conclusion}

On registered constructs, corrected where the registration got them
wrong and audited in public: cooperative in-kernel priority handoff
works on both measured Ampere parts without cluster hardware.
Unloaded notification sits below the experiment's timing resolution,
flat in worker count, on both architectures. Measured handoff
latency tracks the distribution of residual checkpoint work,
predictable held-out from per-block marginals. The runtime safety
contract is CTA-local---a uniform handoff decision, completion
established before reuse---and the coordination obligations split
cleanly: the cross-CTA phase-coherence obligation has no counterpart
when no handoff-sensitive dependency spans CTAs, while the intra-CTA
obligation remains, and our control-divergence faults demonstrate it
directly. Straggler tails are the price, and on these parts buying
bandwidth did not pay them. For the persistent kernels that already
run below Hopper, the scheduler inside is not waiting on hardware;
it is waiting on its slowest tile.
